# Blocking N-glycosylation enhances CAR-T cell efficacy by reprogramming tumor associated myeloid and stromal cells
The tumor microenvironment (TME) hinders the effectiveness of CAR-T cells in solid tumors. Although tumor glycans suppress anticancer immunity, the role of N-glycans on TME cells is unclear. This study shows that the N-glycosylation signature positively correlates with tumor-promoting TME populations in primary tumors and liver metastases of colorectal and pancreatic cancer. Inhibiting N-glycosylation in hepatic stellate cells and M2-like macrophages reduces their ability to support tumor growth and suppress CAR-T cell function. Single-cell RNA sequencing in mice with a humanized immune system reveals that combining CAR-T cells with N-glycosylation inhibitors eliminates pro-tumor IL1b+ macrophages and decreases inhibitory interactions between macrophages and T cells, enhancing anti-tumor responses. Disrupting the MGAT5 gene to block branched N-glycan synthesis in immune and stromal cells boosts CAR-T cell performance regardless of tumor cell glycosylation, indicating a direct beneficial effect of N-glycosylation inhibition on non-malignant TME cells.
